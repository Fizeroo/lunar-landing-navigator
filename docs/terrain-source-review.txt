Quality/coverage cautions
- The 101x101 window is only ~8.08km across; zero NaNs means every raster pixel has a height value, not that each cell is a direct/high-resolution observation. Product 90 exposes LDEC, effective-resolution and height-error companion rasters; this JSON carries none of those quality layers. Describe it as a sampled DEM product with unresolved local effective-resolution/uncertainty, not a uniform 80m-accurate ground truth.
- No terrain hit inside a finite window cannot certify clear visibility. At the center site, distant terrain outside the ~4km half-width can still rise above the ray; smooth-sphere tangent alone does not bound unknown topographic relief. For a horizontal ray, reference-sphere curvature drop is about 260m at 30km distance; any unseen ridge higher than the ray can obstruct. Report unknown/coverage-insufficient unless all relevant terrain is bounded, not only through the local sphere tangent range.
- A blocker inside observed DEM can certify blocked only under the chosen sample/interpolation model. Boundary crossing, pixel footprint, nodata/quality holes, and radial search termination must propagate unknown where they could conceal an obstruction.
- A ray/profile/rendering comparison should consume identical spherical xyz geometry, with origin at actual observer position (R + DEM ground height)u, not the h=0 reference-sphere point. Distinguish app observer height from terrain ground elevation so the center site is not treated as 388.6m above ground.
- A directional horizon/profile should state azimuth binning/resolution and radial extent; a 101-cell grid’s angular gaps/diagonal footprint mean some azimuth ranges terminate sooner than others. Do not treat the square grid as a circularly complete horizon.
